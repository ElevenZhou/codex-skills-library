# Design Direction Library

Use these as starting coordinates, not templates. Adapt the direction to the product, audience, content, and existing brand.

## Direction Selection

Choose one primary direction and one controlled counterpoint. State why it fits the domain before implementation.

| Direction | Works well for | Composition | Type starting point | Motion character | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Editorial authority | Research, publishing, strategy, premium services | Strong columns, pull quotes, image-led pacing, asymmetric rhythm | Newsreader + IBM Plex Sans | Measured reveals and reading progress | Magazine decoration with weak information hierarchy |
| Industrial utility | Operations, infrastructure, logistics, developer tools | Dense grids, rails, strong dividers, compact controls | Archivo + IBM Plex Mono | Direct state transitions with little overshoot | Fake terminal styling and dark-slate sameness |
| Quiet precision | Finance, analytics, professional tools | Tight alignment, deliberate whitespace, fine rules, high data clarity | Instrument Sans + Source Serif 4 | Subtle opacity and positional continuity | Empty luxury spacing that slows repeated work |
| Playful geometry | Education, creator tools, community products | Bold shapes, modular fields, crisp color blocking | Bricolage Grotesque + Noto Sans | Elastic but brief spring motion | Toy-like controls that reduce trust |
| Cultural craft | Food, travel, hospitality, artisan brands | Tactile image crops, layered captions, warm editorial sequencing | Cormorant Garamond + Source Sans 3 | Slow image transitions and restrained parallax | Beige-on-beige lifestyle cliches |
| Technical instrument | Scientific, audio, video, monitoring, engineering | Scales, meters, split panes, high-information surfaces | Chakra Petch + Fira Sans | Responsive controls and continuous feedback | Decorative HUD elements with no function |

## Build a Distinctive Direction

Specify all five dimensions:

1. **Silhouette**: Decide whether the page reads as a rail, canvas, spread, console, timeline, workspace, or stage.
2. **Type voice**: Choose where personality lives: display face, body rhythm, labels, numerals, or monospace data.
3. **Color behavior**: Define dominant neutrals, functional colors, and one accent role. Avoid distributing every color evenly.
4. **Material language**: Choose rules, paper, pixels, metal, ink, fabric, photography, or no material metaphor at all.
5. **Motion grammar**: Choose spring, glide, snap, reveal, continuity, or stillness. Use one grammar consistently.

## Signature Moment Tests

A signature moment should:

- reveal the actual product, object, place, data, or content
- support comprehension or emotional positioning
- survive on mobile without covering essential content
- remain understandable with reduced motion
- be describable in one sentence

Good examples include a live comparison surface, a tactile before/after control, a product-specific data reveal, an image sequence tied to the story, or a distinctive navigation mechanism. A generic glowing button, gradient headline, or floating card is not a signature moment.

## Anti-Repeat Check

Before finalizing a direction, compare it with recent work when context is available. Change at least three of these if the work feels repetitive:

- page silhouette
- type category
- dominant neutral temperature
- accent strategy
- border/radius language
- image treatment
- motion grammar
- navigation model
