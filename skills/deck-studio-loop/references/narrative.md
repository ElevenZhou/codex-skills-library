# Narrative Planning

## Story Rules

- Start with audience tension: why this matters now.
- Move from problem to mechanism to proof to action.
- Make every slide answer a question created by the previous slide.
- Use takeaway titles, not labels.
- Keep visible copy audience-facing; never expose process notes.
- Prefer fewer, stronger claims over many generic bullets.

## Common Arcs

### Product / Company Introduction

1. Category shift or customer pain
2. Product promise
3. Product form
4. Core workflow
5. Architecture / capability system
6. Use cases
7. Business value
8. Differentiation
9. Roadmap
10. Call to action

### Business Plan / Pitch

1. Market inflection
2. Pain and urgency
3. Solution
4. Product demo logic
5. Market and buyer
6. Business model
7. Go-to-market
8. Defensibility
9. Roadmap / milestones
10. Ask / next step

### Technical Product Deck

1. User workflow
2. System boundaries
3. Architecture
4. Data flow
5. Security and governance
6. Reliability and observability
7. Extensibility
8. Deployment model

## Section Rhythm

For decks longer than 12 slides, add chapter divider slides:

- Use large numbers such as `01`, `02`, `03`.
- Use 1 short title and 1 sentence explaining the section job.
- Let chapter slides breathe; do not cram content.
- Repeat a consistent chapter pattern to reduce fatigue.

## Slide Plan Fields

For each slide, define:

- **Takeaway title**
- **Audience question answered**
- **Visual type**: image, table, chart, flow, architecture, case, comparison, chapter
- **Copy budget**: max bullets or max words
- **Source/assumption**
- **QA risk**: dense text, text-in-image, claims, layout complexity
